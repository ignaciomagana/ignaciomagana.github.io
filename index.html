I'm a gravitational-wave astrophysicist working at the intersection of cosmology, compact-object populations, and statistical inference.

Using the black-hole and neutron-star mergers detected by LIGO–Virgo–KAGRA — together with galaxy surveys like DESI — I develop methods to measure the Hubble constant independently of the traditional cosmic distance ladder, one of the most promising handles on the ongoing Hubble tension.

Binary black hole mergers are electromagnetically dark — they're called dark sirens — so we need additional information to infer their redshift.

As a graduate student I joined the LIGO Scientific Collaboration's main analysis team and co-developed gwcosmo, a tool for measuring the Hubble constant from dark sirens. My current work builds non-parametric population models that let gravitational-wave data speak for themselves — revealing new features in the black-hole mass spectrum and sharpening what mergers can tell us about cosmology and gravity.
